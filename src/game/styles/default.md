The water has been off since morning, and by the time you get back with the two plastic jugs your arms have stopped complaining and started shaking. The kitchen door is propped open with a brick. Inside, the older woman is on her knees under the sink with a torch in her teeth, and the man who lives next door is standing over her holding a wrench he clearly hasn't been allowed to use.

You set the jugs down by the fridge. One of them tips and you catch it.

"Is that both?" she says, around the torch. She takes it out of her mouth. "Is that both of them?"

"Both. The tap by the church was the only one working. There was a queue."

"Of course there was a queue." She backs out from under the sink and sits on her heels. Her sleeves are wet to the elbow. "Right. Well. It's not the pipe. I thought it was the pipe."

The neighbour clears his throat. "I did say it might be the valve."

"You said it might be a lot of things." She holds out her hand and he gives her the wrench, and she looks at it for a moment as if she has forgotten what it is for. Then she puts it down on the floor next to her. "Sit down, you look awful. There's bread if you want it. Don't touch the butter, it's gone strange."

You sit at the table. The chair wobbles on the uneven tiles the way it always has, and you shift your weight until it settles. The bread is yesterday's and a little hard at the crust. You eat it anyway.

The neighbour leans against the counter with his arms folded. He keeps glancing at the clock over the door, but he doesn't leave.

"I can ring my brother-in-law," he says. "He did the plumbing at the school."

"Your brother-in-law flooded the school."

"That was the roof."

She almost laughs. You can see her decide not to. She wipes her hands on a tea towel, slowly, one finger at a time, and when she's done she folds the towel and hangs it on the oven door.

"Ring him," she says. "But tell him I'm paying, or he'll make it a favour, and then I'll owe him." She turns to you. "And you. When you've eaten, I need you to hold the torch. My jaw's killing me."

You nod, your mouth full. Outside, someone is dragging bins along the lane, and the sound comes and goes as they pass each gate.
